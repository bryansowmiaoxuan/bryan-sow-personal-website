About

I took the long way round to figuring out what I care about.

I grew up in Singapore, went to Berkeley for EECS, and for a long time chased the version of success that was never meant for me. I thought it was all about achievement and prestige, going after the coveted tech roles and career pathways that everyone was going after. Looking back, a lot of it was insecurity, loneliness and not being honest with myself. Being driven by wanting to belong and prove to myself that I am good enough, drove me to a lot of bad decisions which I genuinely regret.

What actually shifted something was a project with Tukuypaj, a Chilean nonprofit, building a robotic feeding system for patients with quadriplegia. It wasn't some dramatic epiphany in the moment — for once it wasn't about achieving academic or career advancement, it was solely about doing the work in service of someone else's life. Watching someone eat independently because of something I helped build is still the clearest sense I've ever had of what was important to me, to be able to harness STEM for social good, in improving the lives of others.

These days I'm a Technical Product Manager at Enterprise Singapore, building data and AI products, and I still run Operation Einstein on the side — mostly evenings and weekends. I won't pretend I've got it figured out; at times I still feel lost, wanting to find meaningful work that aligns with my values. But at the moment I am contented with what I am doing right now, and in being more deliberate about what I choose to go after, which is more than I could say for a lot of what I used to chase.

Passion Project

Operation Einstein

Hands-on, exploratory STEM education for kids who deserve it most.

Now part of SAAS — building towards a flagship programme

I started Operation Einstein in 2017 when I was in junior college — seventeen years old with a fairly naive but genuine idea: that underprivileged kids deserved the same curious, exploratory science education that wealthier schools take for granted. Not worksheets. Real science you can touch.

Nine years on, we've reached over 1,000 students, built out a chapter model for school partnerships, and developed curriculum I'm genuinely proud of — experiences with structure and intention, not just fun activities for their own sake.

I won't pretend it's been a smooth ride. We've had push back, models that didn't work, and plenty of moments of genuinely wondering whether we were making a difference. But I always hold dear the moments when we do make a difference, and we see the difference we make to the lives of the beneficiaries we meet. We try to care about whether it's working more than we care about being the ones doing it.

More recently, Operation Einstein was acquired by SAAS — the Singapore Association for the Advancement of Science — and we're now working towards becoming one of SAAS's flagship programmes bringing STEM to children from underprivileged backgrounds. I'm truly excited about developing the program under SAAS! Gaining institutional support gives us a real shot and creating a sustainable system that can go on to change the lives of many more beneficiary organisations.
